DKm: the Danish waters and their fjords

A nested pyGETM setup for the Kattegat, the Belt Sea and the western Baltic, with the Danish fjords at high resolution, starting with those of Jutland. A coarse root at 4 km over the Skagerrak and the Kattegat, a Belt Sea level at 700 m, and finer levels down to 60 m for the narrow sounds and the navigation channels of the fjords. The bathymetry is Denmark's Depth Model at 50 m.

Layout

A coarse root and fine levels where the water is narrow

The root covers the Skagerrak up to the Oslo Fjord, the Kattegat, the Belt Sea and the western Baltic, from the Danish North Sea coast to the narrowest section of the Arkona Sea (Kap Arkona–Sweden). Its north edge is the Norwegian coast, not an open boundary across the Skagerrak. At 4 km the Sound is closed at Helsingør and the Little Belt is land: their levels connect them.

The Belt Sea level (700 m, twice as fine as the Kattegat) reaches the root's south and east edges. It covers the whole German Baltic coast and owns the open boundary across the Arkona Sea; the root is masked behind it. The Little Belt, the Sound and the waters south of Sjælland nest in it at 235 m. Each child is a rectangle in its parent's grid; windows may not overlap, so neighbouring fjords share a child where their rectangles would. One row of root water still separates the Belt Sea from the Kattegat, until levels side by side can be coupled directly.

Fjords

The children

North Jutland

  • The Limfjord connects the North Sea (Thyborøn) with the Kattegat (Hals), so the Kattegat level reaches west to Thyborøn and the Limfjord nests in it.
  • The broad western Limfjord at 240 m; the narrow eastern part (300–600 m wide, navigation channel 100–150 m) at 80 m, a third of that.
  • Mariager and Randers Fjord (200–500 m wide, dredged fairways) at 60 m.
  • Their outer parts are 1–2 m deep: these levels need drying and flooding.

The straits and the south

  • Little Belt at 235 m with its fjords in the same level; Middelfart (70 m deep, 700 m wide) sets its time step.
  • The Sound, Helsingør–Falsterbo, with all of Køge Bugt.
  • South of Sjælland: Guldborgsund connected through to the Baltic, with Storstrøm, Grønsund, Ulvsund and Bøgestrøm.
  • Ringkøbing Fjord meets the sea only through the sluice at Hvide Sande: it needs a sluice connection, not ordinary nesting.
Bathymetry

Denmark's Depth Model, completed with EMODnet

Denmark's Depth Model (DDM, version 2.0, Geodatastyrelsen) is a 50 m grid of the Danish waters. It covers the Danish EEZ only, so Swedish, German and Norwegian waters are filled from EMODnet: the DDM is averaged onto a 45 m lon/lat grid, and EMODnet is used only in water outside the DDM's coverage. Inside it, a cell without a DDM value stays land. Every level takes its block means from this one product.

Boundaries

Open boundaries at the model's own points

Two levels have open boundaries: the root on its outer edges (128 points) and the Belt Sea level across the Arkona Sea (100 points at 700 m). The points are the wet cells on those edges that each level computes, taken from the bathymetry, and are kept with the setup as a lon/lat list. As in the OceanICU NSe setup, a boundary file holds the values at exactly these points and pyGETM reads them on its grid, without interpolation. One file serves both levels; each reads its own points from it.

Sources

  • NEAB (all TPXO9 constituents), sampled hourly at the points: elevation and transports.
  • CMEMS with ocean-prep: the North-West Shelf reanalysis (hourly, tidal) and the Baltic reanalysis at Arkona (daily). The Baltic sea level has another datum; it is shifted to the North Sea level as in NSe.
  • TPXO9 directly (it has no data in the Elbe, so that stretch of boundary is dead).

Flather

  • Transports (NEAB, TPXO9): pyGETM's FLATHER_TRANSPORT.
  • Depth-mean velocities (CMEMS): FLATHER_ELEV.
  • The type is taken from the variables in the file.
Tides

First tidal runs: the forcing sets the Kattegat

Ten levels, 2D, one day of spin-up and seven days from 2 January 2020, 64 cores, 82 minutes per run, bottom roughness 0.015 m as in NEAB. M2 from a seven-day fit, at the GESLA gauges from the finest level with water there. The layout is the current one: the Belt Sea has its own open boundary across the Arkona Sea. The third draft, where the root kept that boundary, gave the same at every gauge.

With TPXO9 at its boundaries DKm reproduces the tide of the Kattegat and the Belts. NEAB's tide is twice too large and CMEMS's a third too small, from the Skagerrak inwards. Both come in through the northern part of the west boundary, near the Skagerrak amphidrome: at 57.5–58°N the M2 there is 0.16 m in NEAB and 0.04 m in CMEMS. DKm itself is not the cause. NEAB's error comes from its North Sea (the amphidrome in front of the German Bight, see NEAB). For now TPXO9 is the forcing to use for the tide; CMEMS will be needed for the sea level beyond the tide. The Wadden Sea gauges are poor with every forcing: the root has 4 km cells there. Kiel is too high with NEAB and TPXO9 even with the Kiel Canal closed.

Status

Where it stands

Done

  • Ten levels, evaluated with pyGETM's own time step check; bathymetry of every level from the DDM and EMODnet.
  • Open boundaries at the model's points from NEAB, TPXO9 and CMEMS; a child level with its own open boundary.
  • Eight-day tidal runs of the current layout with three forcings.

Next

  • Drop water cut off from the sea (lagoons, river ends) from the fine levels.
  • Coupling levels side by side (the Belt Sea directly against the Kattegat).
  • The dredged channels as thalwegs in the bathymetry.

Open

  • Sluice connections (Ringkøbing Fjord; Nissum Fjord is not in the DDM).
  • The fjords of the islands (Isefjord, Roskilde Fjord, Odense Fjord, …).
  • The Wadden Sea, with its intertidal flats (also for NEAB).
