DKm: the Danish waters and their fjords

A nested pyGETM setup for the Kattegat, the Belt Sea and the western Baltic, with the Danish fjords at high resolution, starting with those of Jutland. A coarse root at 4 km carries the tides through the straits; finer levels down to 60 m resolve the narrow sounds and the navigation channels of the fjords. The bathymetry is Denmark's Depth Model at 50 m.

Layout

A coarse root and fine levels where the water is narrow

The root covers the Skagerrak, the Kattegat, the Belt Sea and the western Baltic, from the Danish North Sea coast to the narrowest section of the Arkona Sea (Kap Arkona–Sweden, 74 km of open boundary). At 4 km the Sound is closed at Helsingør and the Little Belt is land: their children connect them. Each child is a rectangle in its parent's grid; windows may not overlap, so neighbouring fjords share a child where their rectangles would.

Fjords

The children

North Jutland

  • The Limfjord connects the North Sea (Thyborøn) with the Kattegat (Hals), so the Kattegat level reaches west to Thyborøn and the Limfjord nests in it.
  • The broad western Limfjord at 240 m; the narrow eastern part (300–600 m wide, navigation channel 100–150 m) at 80 m, a third of that.
  • Mariager and Randers Fjord (200–500 m wide, dredged fairways) at 60 m.
  • Their outer parts are 1–2 m deep: these levels need drying and flooding.

The straits and the south

  • Little Belt at 270 m with its fjords in the same level; Middelfart (70 m deep, 700 m wide) sets its time step.
  • The Sound, Helsingør–Falsterbo, with open boundaries to the Kattegat and Køge Bugt.
  • South of Sjælland: Guldborgsund connected through to the Baltic, with Storstrøm, Grønsund, Ulvsund and Bøgestrøm.
  • Ringkøbing Fjord meets the sea only through the sluice at Hvide Sande: it needs a sluice connection, not ordinary nesting.
Bathymetry

Denmark's Depth Model, completed with EMODnet

Denmark's Depth Model (DDM, version 2.0, Geodatastyrelsen) is a 50 m grid of the Danish waters. It covers the Danish EEZ only, so Swedish, German and Norwegian waters are filled from EMODnet: the DDM is averaged onto a 45 m lon/lat grid, and EMODnet is used only in water outside the DDM's coverage. Inside it, a cell without a DDM value stays land. Every level takes its block means from this one product.

Boundaries

Tides from NEAB

In production the root will be forced by CMEMS or another model. For now its open boundaries come from a run of NEAB (all TPXO9 constituents): NEAB writes hourly elevation and transports of its levels, and at every point of the DKm root's grid the finest NEAB level with water there is used. The file has the form of a CMEMS product, a lon/lat grid in time, which pyGETM interpolates to its open boundary points itself; the same code will read CMEMS later.
